Work the In Progress column first, then the cards the board hands out, top of Next first and then Backlog, for as long as there is a card you can take, without stopping for the human. Every card in Next is already the human's order, and an In Progress card is worked only when the human ordered it built (step 2), so nothing here needs a go. Your delegation policy holds in full - the challenge gate, the review and its break and use modes, the round cap, the card comments and the focus - with two changes for this run only, because nobody is watching it:

- A question goes on the card, never to the human. Where the policy would ask with AskUserQuestion, add the question to the card as an Action for Human with `card_add` (list `actions`), which moves it to Blocked by Human, comment with `card_comment` that this drain parked the card on it, and go to the next card. That includes the question after a third review round leaves findings open: the card is parked on it, and no card is filed to hold them. A `Blocker:` line from a subagent parks its card the same way: the `SubagentStop` hook has already added the Action for Human.
- A card leaves your hands at ready to merge. Open its pull request, leave the `ready to merge in PR #<n>` comment the policy gives, and merge nothing: the merge is the human's gate, and the next card is built without it. Releasing it is not this run's either: a release whose annotated tag carries the card on its `Cards:` line moves it from Done to Released once the test gate passes. Under `push: base-only` there is no pull request: leave the `ready to land on <base> at <sha>` comment the policy gives instead, and push and land nothing, because the landing is the human's gate in the same way.

The arguments were `$ARGUMENTS`. A number caps how many cards this run takes, In Progress and handed out together; nothing means no cap. A card in Blocked or Blocked by Human is in neither and stays as it is, and `card_next` never hands one out.

## Procedure

1. List In Progress with `card_list` (`column: "In Progress"`) and record the ids: those are this run's first cards. The rest come one at a time from `card_next`, which gives the top of Next in the human's order, else the first card in Backlog, skipping any card waiting on the human; ask it again after each card you finish or park. A card it hands you that you skip is recorded and not asked for again this run.
2. Take the cards in order. Skip a card, with the reason recorded for the report, when it already has an unticked Action for Human, or when its work depends on an earlier card's pull request that has not merged; `card_read` shows both, and `card_list` with `ready: true` lists the cards whose dependencies are all in Done or Released. A card's own branch is one whose name starts with its id lower-cased followed by `-`, so `cf-184-` is CF-184's and neither `cf-1841-` nor `cf-184.1-` is. Its branches are its own and its sub-issues', every branch whose name starts with its id lower-cased followed by `-` or `.`, so `cf-184.1-` is among CF-184's but never the other way round; the `held` check and resuming read these. Skip an In Progress card, with the reason `not ordered`, unless the human ordered it built, because any agent's start moves a card there from Backlog as well as from Next. Any one of these, on the card itself, is the order: a lead `Sub-issue <n> of <m>: started` comment; the human's recorded order; its own branch or a worktree on it, because the fleet cuts one only for a build the human ordered, while a sub-issue's branch orders that sub-issue alone and never its parent; or the board's event trail showing the card moved into In Progress from Next, which `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/boards/boards.sh history <id>` prints. A card `card_next` hands you from Next is ordered; one from Backlog is built only when the same evidence shows the human ordered it, and is otherwise skipped with the reason `not ordered`. Skip one, with the reason `held`, when something still holds it, so two builders never share a branch: a spawn on it is still running, or a worktree under `.claude/worktrees/` on one of its branches, found with `git worktree list --porcelain`, is locked by a live process. The harness's lock reason carries a pid, and `kill -0 <pid>` says whether it is live, as `prune-worktrees` reads it. A lock whose process is gone does not hold the card, and you never unlock it.
3. Build each card you take as your policy says, with the two changes above, until it is ready to merge or parked. Bind each build task to its card with the `[board:<id>]` marker in the task's subject, as the policy says. Resume on progress, not on the column: a card whose comments carry `Sub-issue <n> of <m>:` progress, or that has branches, worktrees or an open pull request, is picked up from where those say it stands, in the worktree it already has rather than a new one, and never rebuilt; a card with no progress starts fresh. A `ready to merge in PR #<n>` comment marks one sub-issue, and so does a `ready to land on <base> at <sha>` comment, under which ready to land and landed count as ready to merge and merged below. Read the latest comment's `Done still needs:` part and the sub-issue cards: the card counts as ready to merge only when every sub-issue is ready to merge or merged and the card names each PR; otherwise build the next sub-issue not yet built. Only a card step 2 counted as ordered from its own evidence gets its next sub-issue built, because ordering a card orders its parts; a card taken only through one sub-issue's order builds that sub-issue and none of its siblings. Judge done against the card's criteria, never by how many sub-issues have merged. When every sub-issue is ready to merge or merged but the card does not name each PR, rebuild nothing: find each missing number on the sub-issue's card or with `gh pr list --state all --head <branch>` on its branch, add them to the card in one comment, and count the card ready to merge. Under `push: base-only` the card names each sub-issue's ready-to-land head instead, read with `git rev-parse <branch>` on its branch, never `gh pr list`. Then clear the focus with `card_focus` and no id before the next card, so nothing of this card binds the next one's spawns.
4. Stop when there is no card left to take, when the cap is reached, or when a failure is not the card's own - the board's MCP server gone, the gates red on the base branch, every spawn returning without a handoff - and say which.
5. End with one line, exactly in this shape, writing an empty group as `0 parked (-)` and so on:

   `In Progress drained: <n> ready to merge (<ids>), <n> parked (<ids>), <n> skipped (<ids>), <n> not reached (<ids>). Next drained: <n> ready to merge (<ids>), <n> parked (<ids>), <n> skipped (<ids>), <n> not reached (<ids>).`

   The Next group counts every card `card_next` handed out, Backlog cards included.

## Running it unattended

One pass can end before the list does, when the context fills or a turn ends early. `/goal` keeps it going; what it does and what it cannot do are under Goals in the `lead-procedures` skill. In a session whose agent is the lead, as the project settings make it, switch to auto mode so tool calls do not wait on a prompt, then set:

```text
/goal Use the coder-fleet:drain skill to work the In Progress column and then the cards card_next hands out, top of Next then Backlog. Done when the transcript's last "Next drained:" line, which follows an "In Progress drained:" segment, accounts for every In Progress card the drain listed at its start and every card card_next handed it, each one ready to merge with its pull request named on the card, parked with an open Action for Human on the card, or skipped with a reason, and card_next has nothing left that the drain has not recorded. Or stop after 150 turns.
```

What comes back for the human in the morning is the drained line, the pull requests to merge and the Blocked by Human column.
