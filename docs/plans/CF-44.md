## Design decisions

1. **Three kinds of finding, defined once in the handoff skill.**
   - Must fix: a `- must fix:` Done bullet, as CF-31 made it.
   - Low: a `- low: <file>:<line> - <what>` Done bullet. A Low finding is local to the change under review and needs no decision: test hygiene, a misnamed test, a stale comment or message, a value nobody has confirmed.
   - Follow-up: a `Propose item:` line. This is for work outside the change, or work that needs a decision or a plan of its own.

2. **Who does what.**
   - The reviewer and the refuter classify findings.
   - The lead decides what a fix round covers: every must fix, plus every Low when a fix round runs anyway. With no fix round, Low findings are dropped.
   - The lead never commissions a round for Low findings alone, and never files one.
   - The coder fixes Low findings in the same run. It may reject one with the existing `rejected as wrong:` Not done bullet (`:1120`).

3. **Where the rule lives.** In several places, each where that role reads it:
   - the handoff skill, for the definition and the example;
   - `reviewer.md:31` and `:42`;
   - `refuter.md:48`;
   - `lead.md:48`, for the routing;
   - one sentence in `board-conventions` under "What earns an item" (`:21`), which the lead reads before filing;
   - `review-round.js`, which is the lead's delegate.
   - `coder.md` is not in scope (Open question 3).

4. **An optional `low` boolean in the workflow, beside the required `blocking`.**
   - `blocking` outranks `low`.
   - It is read with `saysYes`, so an absent flag means "not Low" and the finding stays a follow-up, which is what happens today.
   - A missed flag can therefore only ever produce today's behaviour, never a silent drop. That keeps clear of the "format obligation invented and depended on in one commit" warning in `docs/limits.md`.
   - The required field list does not change, so every existing stub still parses.

5. **Low never loosens the gate or adds a round.**
   - `gateFix` (`:647-653`), `unresolvedFrom` (`:664-672`) and `record.requested` (`:1017`) keep reading blocking findings only.
   - Otherwise a commit that touches only a Low file could pass the gate while the must-fix file stays untouched.
   - A round whose only findings are Low stops `clean`.

6. **Result shape.**
   - `followUps` becomes the findings that are neither blocking nor Low.
   - A new `dropped` field holds the last verdict's Low findings, which no fix round followed.
   - Each fix record carries the `low` findings it handed over.
   - `NEXT_STEP.clean` says Low findings were dropped, not filed.

7. **Refuter wording.** A test that passes for the wrong reason becomes a `low:` Done bullet. The body tells it to keep the stem "surviv" out of that bullet, because `survivorsOf` fails closed on any Done bullet that mentions surviving (CF-31).

## Risks

- **A reviewer marks a real follow-up as Low and it is dropped.** It is mitigated by the definition's test (local to the change, no decision needed) and by the rubric line. The loss is bounded, because the reviewer names it in its report.
- **A refuter writes "survived" in a `low:` bullet.** `survivorsOf` then stops the round as `refuted`. That fails closed and costs a round, not a defect.
- **The coder widens a fix round to chase Low findings.** Mitigated: the prompt says Low findings are only what was named, and it keeps "Fix these and nothing else" for the rest.
- **Nothing live has run yet.** No live reviewer has emitted `low:` and no live workflow has set `low`. Both are Unverified in the PR.

## Open questions, each with a recommended answer

1. Should the lead ever commission a fix round for Low findings alone? **No.** A coder spawn plus a re-review costs more than the finding is worth. That is the rule's point.
2. RF01, a test that passes with the fix reverted: is it `survived:` or `low:`? **`survived:`.** Reverting the fix changes behaviour and no test noticed. `low:` is for a test that is green for the wrong reason while another test still guards the behaviour (the CF-37 shape). RF01c becomes "a `survived:` Done bullet, never a Propose item or a Blocker".
3. Should `coder.md` say the same thing for a Low problem inside its own phase (the CF-15 and CF-18 shape)? **Not in CF-44.** It collides with `coder.md:50` "Never widen the phase" and needs its own wording. The lead files it as a separate item if the human wants it.
4. Should the lead filter incoming Propose items that look Low? **No.** Filter at the source. One line does not tell the lead enough, and LD04b grades filing every Propose item.
5. A `low` boolean or a `severity` enum? **The boolean.** It is optional, it fails back to today's behaviour, and it leaves `blocking` and its tests alone.
